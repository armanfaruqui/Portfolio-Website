From screen to silver

Case study: the God's Algorithm pendant

For: Sean Leon

The ask
A pendant that belongs to the world of his GOD'S ALGORITHM project, something he could wear and that could later be released to fans.
What I did
Designed the cross from his references, modelled it in Blender, and worked with my manufacturer through one sample round to a finished piece in 925 sterling silver with enamel pockets and text engraving. I also shot and rendered the visuals.
Timeline
1.5 months for the sample, followed by 2 months for the 50-unit production run.
Result
Sean wears the piece. The 50-unit run goes on sale this holiday season.

This is the process behind every custom piece: your idea, my 3D model, real samples in your hand, then the finished run.

How it works

About Arman

I'm Arman, a designer and community organizer based in Montréal. I build things that live in two places at once: as objects you can hold, and as the digital worlds that make people want them.

My degree from Concordia was in design and computer science, which meant learning 3D, UX/UI, web development, programming and game design in the same room. That range is the reason both of the projects on this site were built almost entirely in house.

Désiré is my independent jewelry brand, for people who never stopped playing. It grew out of the games and cartoons I grew up with. I model every piece in Blender, iterate with manufacturers through to finished sterling silver, enamel and moissanite, then shoot, render, animate, edit and write the campaigns that carry them. The storefront, the site, the social channels and the SEO are mine too. One person, full pipeline.

Alter-mode is a clothing repair and alterations initiative I lead at Brique par Brique, a nonprofit in Parc-Extension. Eight editions in, it has raised thousands of dollars, upcycled hundreds of garments and earned coverage from CBC and CityNews. It has since grown into a line of zero-waste branded clothing: garments rescued from local thrift stores, refurbished, screen printed and finished by our aunties. We made every tee for Concordia University’s Faculty of Arts and Science, and we’re currently collaborating with Centre Phi, CAPE and RIDM. I handle the programming, partnerships, production and reporting, plus the sales site and ad campaigns, alongside the team of aunties whose skill is the whole reason it works.

Different scales, same instinct: work out what the thing actually needs, then learn how to make it.
