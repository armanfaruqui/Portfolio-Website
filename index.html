The God's Algorithm pendant

For Sean Leon

The ask
A pendant that belongs to the world of his GOD'S ALGORITHM project, something he could wear and that could later be released to fans.
What I did
Designed the cross from his references, modelled it in Blender, and worked with my manufacturer through one round of samples to a finished piece in 925 sterling silver with enamel pockets and text engraving. I also shot and rendered the visuals.
Timeline
Six weeks from first sketch to the sample, then two months for the 50-unit production run.
Result
Sean wears the piece. The 50-unit run goes on sale this holiday season.

Alter-mode pop-ups

With Brique par Brique, a nonprofit in Parc-Extension, Montréal

The ask
Get a neighbourhood to repair clothes instead of throwing them out, and pay skilled immigrant and asylum-seeking women fairly to do it.
What I did
Built the format, the look, the partnerships and the promotion, and ran each event with the team of aunties whose skill is the reason it works.
Result
Eight editions, hundreds of garments upcycled, covered by CBC and CityNews. It has since grown into a branded clothing line with orders from Concordia, Centre Phi, CAPE and RIDM.

About Arman

I’m Arman, a designer and community organizer in Montréal. I make things you can hold, and the films, sites and events that get people to care about them.

I grew up in Karachi on anime and video games, then studied design and computer science at Concordia, where 3D, web, programming and game design were taught in the same room. That mix is why I can take a project from the first sketch to the finished thing without handing it off.

Désiré is my jewelry brand, for people who never stopped playing. I model each piece in Blender, work with manufacturers until it exists in sterling silver, enamel and moissanite, then shoot, animate and write the campaigns around it. I built the shop and run the channels too.

Alter-mode is a clothing repair pop-up I lead at Brique par Brique, a nonprofit in Parc-Extension. Over eight editions it has upcycled hundreds of garments and been covered by CBC and CityNews. It has grown into a line of zero-waste branded clothing: garments rescued from local thrift stores, then screen printed and finished by our aunties, whose skill is the reason it works. We made 400 tees for Concordia’s Faculty of Arts and Science and are now working with Centre Phi, CAPE and RIDM. I look after the events, partnerships, production, site and campaigns.

Both began as a file on my laptop and ended up in strangers’ hands. Getting from one to the other is the part I’m good at, and it’s what I do for other people’s projects too: product films, custom jewelry, and pop-ups and installations. See Collaborate.
